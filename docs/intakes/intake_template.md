# Research Session Intake Worksheet

> **Purpose:** Capture one research conversation/session in a structured form that another LLM can use later without needing access to the original chat.
>
> **Scope:** Create **one intake file per session** and store completed files under:
>
> `docs/intakes/`
>
> Recommended filename:
>
> `YYYY-MM-DD_HHMM_<short-session-topic>.md`
>
> Example:
>
> `2026-09-16_2145_route-gradient-idea.md`

---

## Instructions for the LLM filling this worksheet

Use the conversation/session as the primary source.

Your job is to **capture the researcher's thinking faithfully**, not to improve the science or invent a stronger paper.

### Do

- Preserve the researcher's terminology, uncertainty, priorities, and intent.
- Separate observations, hypotheses, questions, literature beliefs, decisions, and proposed directions.
- Record what the researcher actually has: code, data, figures, papers, experiments, notes, hardware, constraints.
- Preserve conflicting ideas if the researcher has not resolved them.
- Record important negative information: ideas rejected, approaches that failed, questions already answered, or things the researcher explicitly does not want.
- Include enough context that another LLM can understand the session without reading the original conversation.
- Prefer concise, information-dense notes over polished prose.
- Quote or closely preserve the researcher's own phrasing when it captures the intuition better than a summary.
- Mark anything inferred by you rather than explicitly stated by the researcher.
- Leave sections empty or write `None stated` when the session does not contain the information.

### Do not

- Invent a research gap.
- Claim novelty.
- Promote a hypothesis to a fact.
- Treat the researcher's belief about the literature as verified literature evidence.
- Invent experimental results, citations, implementation details, or motivations.
- Resolve contradictions silently.
- Rewrite the session into a paper narrative unless the researcher explicitly did that in the conversation.
- Turn tentative ideas into commitments.
- Decide which scientific interpretation is correct.
- Make new scientific decisions on the researcher's behalf.

### Status vocabulary

Use these tags where helpful:

- `[OBSERVATION]` — something the researcher reports actually observing
- `[HYPOTHESIS]` — a proposed explanation
- `[QUESTION]` — unresolved
- `[UNVERIFIED-BACKGROUND]` — something believed about prior work but not verified in this session
- `[IDEA]` — possible direction or experiment
- `[DECISION]` — a choice explicitly made by the researcher
- `[CONSTRAINT]` — practical or scientific restriction
- `[ASSET]` — existing code, data, figures, papers, hardware, or notes
- `[REJECTED]` — idea or direction explicitly ruled out
- `[INFERENCE]` — interpretation added by the LLM; use sparingly
- `[TODO]` — concrete next action mentioned or implied by the session

Do **not** use `[FACT]` or `[CLAIM]` unless the researcher explicitly labels something that way and provides supporting evidence in the session.

## Final instruction to downstream agents

This intake is a **session record**, not an authoritative statement of scientific truth.

Before treating anything here as established:

1. distinguish observations from hypotheses;
2. verify literature beliefs against source material;
3. verify experimental claims against project artifacts;
4. defer to newer approved project-state, ground-truth, claim-evidence, and decision-log files when conflicts exist.

Do not silently promote tentative material from this intake into manuscript claims.
